# Governance

Version 0.1

This file governs changes to the New Jurisdiction experiment. It governs repository decisions; it does not establish a real government.

## Roles

### Contributors

Anyone may open an Issue, propose a change or participate in review, subject to the repository contribution rules and hosting platform policies. A contributor may be human-only, use AI assistance or submit agent-produced work with accurate disclosure.

### Reviewers

Any contributor may review a proposal. Review is substantive when it identifies a concrete drafting, dependency, evidence, rights, institutional or case-application issue and explains why it matters.

### Maintainers

For v0.1, Legal Machines maintainers administer the repository, determine whether review is sufficient, record decisions and merge accepted changes. This is transparent maintainer governance, not a claim of democratic representation.

Maintainers may not treat their status, an AI output or Legal Machines software as a substitute for reasons.

## Proposal lifecycle

1. **Problem identification.** A contributor selects an open question or opens an Issue describing a bounded legal problem.
2. **Current-state review.** The contributor reads the Charter, relevant accepted law, related proposals, cases and research.
3. **Proposal.** The contributor opens a branch and Pull Request containing a structured proposal record plus the smallest necessary changes.
4. **Review.** Humans and AI systems may test assumptions, dependencies, drafting and cases. Material AI review must be disclosed.
5. **Revision.** The proposer answers or incorporates objections without silently changing the proposal's objective.
6. **Decision.** A maintainer accepts, rejects or requests further work and records the principal reasons in the Pull Request.
7. **Record.** Accepted changes are merged. Rejected or withdrawn proposals remain inspectable in the closed Pull Request and its discussion, subject to the hosting platform's retention behavior.

Opening a proposal does not create a presumption that it will be accepted. Popularity, model confidence and volume of generated review are not decision rules.

## Review standard

Before accepting a material proposal, maintainers should determine whether the record adequately addresses:

- compatibility with the Charter;
- a clear problem and objective;
- affected provisions and dependencies;
- relevant interests and trade-offs;
- the strongest objection;
- meaningful concrete cases;
- cited support and source limits;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- accurate authorship disclosure;
- deterministic repository checks.

Passing validation is necessary for repository integrity but says nothing by itself about legal merit.

## Decisions and conflicts

Maintainers decide by reasoned judgment, not token voting or automatic model scoring. When reviewers disagree, the decision record should identify the material disagreement and explain why the accepted position was chosen or why no change was accepted.

A maintainer with a material personal or financial conflict should disclose it and, where another maintainer is available, refrain from being the sole decision-maker. If recusal is not possible, the limitation must be stated in the decision record.

Abusive conduct, fabricated evidence, undisclosed automated spam and changes outside the stated scope may be closed without substantive adoption review. Good-faith criticism of the project, maintainers or proposals is not grounds for rejection.

## Charter amendments

A Charter amendment must:

- identify itself as a Charter amendment in the Issue, proposal record and Pull Request title;
- explain why ordinary law or governance changes are insufficient;
- describe the effect on the experiment's purpose, limits and participation model;
- receive explicit adversarial review focused on the Charter consequences;
- be merged only with a written maintainer decision.

No ordinary proposal may silently override the Charter.

## Governance amendments

This governance model may evolve through a Pull Request following the ordinary proposal process. A governance proposal must explain the real coordination problem it solves and the accountability trade-offs it introduces.

The project will not invent elections, representative claims, token voting or automated governance before a real need and legitimate basis exist.

## Publication controls

Normal contributions enter through Pull Requests rather than direct changes to `main`. Branch protection and other hosting controls are operational settings, not facts established by this file. They must not be claimed as active unless they have actually been configured and verified.
